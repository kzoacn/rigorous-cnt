# Algorithms 3–6: implementation specification and mathematical checks

## Independent residue calculation

Completeness checking needs an interval for h_K R_K. The code implements [Belabas–Friedman, *Computing the residue of the Dedekind zeta function*, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.0035), providing the residue-approximation interface required by Proposition 2.9 with an explicit error bound.

Finite prime-power sums and directed ball arithmetic enclose log(rho_K). The class number formula then gives an interval for h_K R_K. The theorem supplies an analytic error bound conditional on GRH for zeta_K and zeta_Q. Numerical stability between consecutive truncation points does not replace that bound.

The required operations are prime-ideal decomposition, discriminant and signature computation, and roots of unity. The default solving path does not call `pari_bnf`, `class_group`, `unit_group`, or `S_unit_group`. Tests disable `pari_bnf` while solving and call Sage's group solver for comparison only afterwards.

Roots of unity are returned separately because the Log map annihilates them. The complete S-unit group is generated by the returned free generators together with all roots of unity.

## Algorithm 3 parameters and specialized sampling

The block size is `ceil(n^(2/3))`, clamped to `[2,n]`. Choose rho_tilde from the certified residue interval so that `rho_tilde <= rho_K < 2*rho_tilde`. Then use Algorithm 3's threshold to select either the trivial modulus or a product of small prime ideals. A nontrivial modulus is squarefree. `modulus_override` supplies an explicit modulus for exercising the exceptional-unit branch. The report records the override without attributing it to the paper's automatic residue-based choice.

For Theorem 13.2, tau is drawn uniformly from `(O_K/m0)^*`: sample separately in the HNF quotient-lattice box for each prime power, reject elements lying in its underlying prime ideal, and combine by CRT. Each local acceptance probability is at least 1/2. With trivial finite modulus, tau is 1 and there are no real sign restrictions.

Choose a dyadic epsilon at most `1/(1200*abs(Delta)*r^n)`; delta and distortion follow Algorithm 2. For each Algorithm 3 candidate, check whether `(alpha)/a` is an integral ideal supported on the factor base, and reconstruct the exact identity on success.

For the actual factor base, the prime-count conditions in Section 14's combinatorial proof can be checked directly, without guessing big-O constants: the number of available prime ideals of norm at most B must be at least `B/(4 log B)`, and `r^n >= e^n B`. Also check that the factor base contains every prime-ideal factor used by the random walk. Together with a valid mixing bound these imply a conditional success-probability lower bound. Without verified mixing, the report retains the conditional bound without labeling the success probability as established.

## Normalizations that preserve the distribution

Repeated relation sampling can produce large ideals and rational scales. The specialized sampler reduces arithmetic cost through two exact changes of variables:

- If `a=c*a0`, with c a positive rational that is a unit at the modulus, use a0 and tau/c, then multiply the returned element by c. Lattices, boxes, and membership conditions are scaled together, preserving the original output distribution.
- Remove a common positive rational factor from y, using the norm independence in Lemma 9.4. Restore the scaled point and radius in the returned record.

Prime decompositions and fixed discretization parameters are also cached. Caching changes computation cost only.

## Discretized divisors in Algorithm 4

Finite coordinates lie in Z^S and infinite coordinates in `(1/N)Z^(r1+r2)`. Round N up to at least the paper's `omega^n*exp(11+16 log²|Delta|+9n²)` bound. The Gaussian uses Lemma 2.22 with error budget 1/100.

After sampling finite valuations a_P and infinite coordinates b_nu, compute a rational y approximating `exp(b_nu/w_nu)`, where w_nu is 1 at a real place and 2 at a complex place. Certify absolute error at most `min(z_nu)/N`. The input ideal `product(P^a_P)` may be fractional; Algorithms 1–3 support this case.

Algorithm 3 then returns alpha and v_P. The resulting S-unit valuations are a_P+v_P, and the Log-S-unit row is `(-(a_P+v_P), Log(alpha))`, matching the paper's signs.

`generation_radius` is a candidate upper bound for the generation radius. The chosen Gaussian width satisfies `sigma >= 3 max(sqrt(log(dim)), generation_radius)`. Once complete generation is established, check the candidate bound against the actual basis. If it fails, the separate completeness evidence remains valid, but the corresponding generation-distribution theorem's premise is not claimed to hold.

## BKP and precision control

Use a rational lower bound for Kessler's explicit minimum-length bound `1/(1000 sqrt(n) log(n)^3)`. It also bounds every Log-S-unit sublattice: a nonzero finite coordinate has length at least 1, while a vector with all finite coordinates zero belongs to the ordinary logarithmic unit lattice.

Each BKP pass uses C, lambda, T, and q from Lemma 23.4, certifies the row-norm approximation error, and constructs the integer matrix `[2I | round(2^(q+1) A_tilde)]`. Retain its LLL unimodular transformation. When there are more real coordinates than rows, add zero rows to meet the lemma's dimension condition and remove their auxiliary coefficients from the output.

Use the lemma's length thresholds to identify zero relations and basis rows, with additional checks:

- The transformation is unimodular and exactly reconstructs the augmented matrix.
- Each proposed zero relation has an actual logarithmic-vector length strictly below the nonzero-length lower bound, so it must be zero.
- Ball arithmetic proves that the remaining rows have a positive Gram determinant.

After obtaining a basis, apply the same construction to it again, following the two-pass procedure of Theorem 23.5. Each pass requests original data at sufficient precision instead of treating an approximate intermediate matrix as exact. Numerical reduction failure at fpLLL's `eta=1/2` boundary triggers a fallback to exact NTL LLL.

Logarithms of compact products increase working precision to account for exponent bit lengths and cancellation, then check the final interval widths. Fixed 128-bit working precision is not assumed sufficient for cancellation involving integer exponents hundreds of bits long.

## Class-generation hypotheses and volume conventions

The completeness criterion requires the working factor base to generate the ordinary class group. Enumerate the necessary small prime ideals up to the Minkowski bound. Each must either be in the factor base or have an exact relation expressing its class in terms of that base.

If a missing small prime divides m0, an Algorithm 5 exceptional relation can be computed in advance as its witness and reused after completing the ordinary T-unit group. The stopping criterion therefore does not begin with an unproved class-generation assumption.

In the implemented coordinates `(-v_P, Log(alpha))`, the normal to the degree-zero hyperplane is `(log N(P),...,1,...,1)`. The complete-lattice covolume to compare with the actual Euclidean Gram determinant is therefore:

```text
h_K * R_K * sqrt(r1+r2 + sum_P log(N(P))^2).
```

The expression `h_K*R_K*sqrt(r1+r2)` in Lemma 15.5 of the paper corresponds to a normalized quantity. The implementation includes the slope factor converting the two volume conventions, rather than mixing the normalized quantity with a Euclidean Gram determinant. For example, for `K=Q(i)` and `S={(3)}`, the generator 3 gives the vector `(-1, log 9)`, whose Euclidean length is `sqrt(1+log(9)^2)`. A regression test covers this case.

Divide the candidate-lattice covolume interval by the independently computed full-lattice interval. The true ratio must be a positive integer. Declare completeness only when the possible integer indices form exactly `{1}`; full rank alone is insufficient. Tests include a full-rank subgroup of index two.

## Algorithms 5–6 and arbitrary S

Algorithm 5 calls the sampler with input ideal q and modulus `m0/q` until `(alpha)/q` is supported on T. It checks that the valuation at q is exactly one.

Algorithm 6 repeats Algorithm 4 and the BKP/volume checks. Once a complete T-unit basis is found, it adds the generators for all exceptional primes q. The working set also contains enough Minkowski-bound primes and all primes actually used by the walk, ensuring class-group coverage and keeping the walk's prime factors inside the factor base.

For an arbitrary requested S, take the saturated integer kernel of the valuation matrix at extra primes and apply it to the compact exponent matrix. Approximate real columns are not subjected to HNF, and valuations are not discarded because a floating-point value is close to zero. The returned rank is `|S|+r1+r2-1`.

After finding a complete T-unit basis, its images in `(O_K/m0)^*` can also check whether T generates the ray class group. Once ordinary class generation is established, the remaining condition is surjectivity of the residue images of T-units. This check uses integer HNF for a finite group and does not call an existing S-unit solver.

## Compact representation and recovery of small results

Outputs retain base elements and integer exponents. Direct expansion with large exponents can produce enormous intermediate coefficients even when the final element is a small unit.

`materialize` first uses exact valuations to choose a denominator d such that d*beta is integral. Certified logarithmic embeddings and the inverse integral-basis embedding matrix then bound all its integral-basis coefficients. Evaluate the power product modulo a sufficiently large power of a small prime and recover the unique bounded integer coefficients. Negative exponents are converted to exact inverse field elements, avoiding unnecessary inversion in a polynomial ring over a composite modulus. The modulus is coprime to all required denominators.

Recovery has an output bit-size budget. If the result is too large, the element remains compact and a resource limit is reported. Tests include nontrivial cancellation with exponents of size 10^50, fractional results, roots-of-unity phases, and a non-power integral basis.

## Limits of the guarantees

- Exact ideal identities, integer transformations, and restriction to arbitrary S can be checked individually.
- Complete generation depends on the GRH hypotheses of the explicit residue bound.
- Default reference parameters are not claimed to satisfy a general walk-mixing bound. This affects sampling-time guarantees; the separate completeness evidence at termination remains valid.
- A certified mixing-parameter factory can be supplied explicitly. The existing spectral calibrator needs additional class/unit preprocessing, whose cost and dependencies must be reported separately.
- The entire paper's bit-complexity guarantees have not been established. Nonmaximal orders and large-scale computations in high-degree fields are outside the validated scope. Exhausting a resource budget is not interpreted as having found a complete group.
